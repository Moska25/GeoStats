1What the earnings figures actually measure

Gross, before personal income tax. Nothing in GeoStats is take-home pay. Converting to net would need each year's income-tax and pension-contribution rules, which this project does not model.

An average over paid employees. Geostat computes it as the earnings fund divided by the average number of paid employees divided by the number of months. It is not the salary of a typical person: it excludes the self-employed and the unemployed entirely, and it averages over everyone who is on a payroll.

Mean and median come from different sources. The mean is from the enterprise survey. The median is from Revenue Service administrative records and only starts in 2018. They are shown side by side because the gap between them is the point: the mean is dragged upward by high earners, so it always sits above the middle earner. If someone asks "what does a normal job pay", the median is the closer answer, and neither is take-home.

No distribution is published. A mean and a median cannot be inverted into percentiles. The analyst refuses those questions rather than modelling an answer.

2The currency-era warning

read this one

The annual earnings workbook carries this footnote under the title:

*Currency - before 1993 -Rouble; 1993 - Coupon; 1994 - Thousand Coupon; since 1995 - Lari.

Columns from {{ wage_first }} to 1994 are therefore denominated in three currencies that no longer exist, and only {{ gel_era_start }} onward is in Lari. The columns sit adjacent in a single sheet row with no visual break. Reading the sheet as one series produces a 1995 figure of 13.5 next to a 1994 figure of 6,151.6 — an apparent 99.8% collapse in wages that is entirely a redenomination.

GeoStats handles it in three places:

  • the parser tags every value with the currency in force in that year (adapters.currency_for_year), so the unit is part of the composite key;
  • the CURRENCY_ERA contract fails any series that carries more than one era under one unit label, and the fault lab has a Mislabel the currency era injection that proves it fires;
  • the explorer defaults to {{ gel_era_start }} onward and warns loudly if you widen the range past the boundary.

There is no conversion factor in these files, and GeoStats will not invent one. Pre-1995 values are shown in their own currency or not at all.

3Formulas

CPI base and rebasing. The price index used throughout is Geostat's consumer price index with 2010 annual average = 100. GeoStats derives an annual average by taking the arithmetic mean of the twelve published monthly indices, and only for years where all twelve months exist — a partial year would understate the average and silently bias every real figure. The derivation is checkable: it returns exactly 100.0 for 2010, which is the base year by construction. Coverage runs {{ cpi_first }}–{{ cpi_last }}. Deflating to a different base year is a rebasing, not a recomputation: the explorer's base-year selector divides through by a different CPI value and leaves the underlying series alone.

6Limitations

  • Earnings before 1995 are in Rouble, Coupon or Thousand Coupon and are not comparable with anything after; no conversion is applied.
  • The CPI series starts in {{ cpi_first }}, so wage years {{ wage_first }}–1999 cannot be deflated at all. The JOIN contract fails on exactly those years rather than pretending they are covered.
  • The median series starts in 2018 and is published by economic activity only — never by region, sex or ownership.
  • Earnings by activity exist under two incompatible classifications (NACE rev.1 to 2019, NACE rev.2 from 2014). GeoStats keeps them as separate breakdowns and does not splice them.
  • Regional figures place enterprises at the head-office location, which overstates Tbilisi relative to where work is physically done.
  • The 2006 column in the activity and sex files carries a methodology footnote about financial-sector coverage; the drop there is a definition change, not a wage fall.
  • The latest annual earnings figure is marked **, preliminary. It is expected to be revised.
  • Only the headline Total line of the year-on-year CPI file is ingested; the full COICOP tree is not.
  • Employment, unemployment and the quarterly earnings releases are not ingested at all.
  • Georgian covers {{ '%.0f'|format(ka_coverage * 100) }}% of the interface strings. Explanatory prose stays in English deliberately — a half-correct translation of a statistical caveat is worse than none.
