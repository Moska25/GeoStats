What these numbers are. Geostat earnings are gross, before personal income tax, and are an average over paid employees (earnings fund ÷ average paid employees ÷ months). They are not take-home pay and not a typical person's salary. The mean is pulled up by high earners, which is why the median is shown next to it.

The trap: four currencies in one row of columns

the reason this project exists

The annual earnings workbook carries a footnote: “Currency — before 1993 Rouble; 1993 Coupon; 1994 Thousand Coupon; since 1995 Lari.” The columns are therefore not one series. Plot them naively and 1995 looks like a 99.8% wage collapse. It was a currency reform. GeoStats tags every value with the currency actually in force that year, the CURRENCY_ERA contract fails any series that mixes eras under one label, and the explorer defaults to the Lari era.

Why two lines. The distance between them is inflation. Real index = (nominalt / nominalbase) ÷ (CPIt / CPIbase) × 100, using the Geostat CPI with 2010 annual average = 100. The CPI annual average is derived here from the twelve published monthly indices, and only for years where all twelve are present. That is why the real line starts at {{ cpi_first }} even though nominal earnings go back to {{ wage_first }}.

The labour market beside real wages

Open the work page

Unemployment here is the ILO definition, ages 15 and over. A person counts as unemployed only if they are out of work, available for work and actively looking for it; someone who has stopped looking is outside the labour force rather than unemployed. It comes from the Labour Force Survey, which asks households where they live — a different instrument and a different population from the earnings series above, which counts payroll at the employer. The two are shown together because they are both about work, not because one explains the other.
